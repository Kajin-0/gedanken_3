Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript **“Task-dependent photodetector ordering under unknown arrival time”** for publication as a Research Article in *Applied Optics*.

The manuscript examines a detector-qualification question that arises in transient optical measurements when the event arrival time is uncertain. We construct a controlled causal optical-to-electrical detector family whose members have equal eventual matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio for the same optical event but different response times. A shorter response time accumulates evidence sooner, yet it also shortens the timing-scan correlation length and therefore enlarges the normalized search over a fixed physical arrival-time window. Under one global-false-alarm batch protocol, these competing effects produce task-dependent sufficient-guarantee-time ordering.

The paper gives both a general ordering result and a finite continuous-time witness. In the witness, the faster channel is preferred when arrival time is known, while at the same finite physical timing uncertainty the slower channel remains guarantee-feasible and the faster channel is already guarantee-infeasible. The finite result is established with one-sided Rice and Slepian bounds rather than a discretized timing-search approximation.

We believe the work is appropriate for *Applied Optics* because it connects photodetector temporal response to a concrete optical acquisition/decision protocol and clarifies what additional task information is needed before detector “speed” can be translated into time to a specified detection operating point. The manuscript explicitly distinguishes this result from established sensitivity-bandwidth metrics and classical unknown-delay acquisition theory.

The manuscript does **not** claim a universal preference for slower detectors, does not claim an exact full signal-present scan-time reversal, and does not claim priority for the underlying acquisition/search-penalty ingredients.
